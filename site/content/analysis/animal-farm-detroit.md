## The Seven Commandments of Purpose Charter Academy

*Painted on the barn wall. Revised nightly.*

~~1. All children shall receive an education.~~
1. All children shall receive a **certificate of attendance**.

~~2. No administrator shall have a criminal record.~~
2. No administrator shall have **fewer than nine** convictions.

~~3. The per-pupil funding belongs to the students.~~
3. The per-pupil funding belongs to **The Purpose Group, LLC**.

~~4. Every dollar shall be accounted for.~~
4. Every dollar shall be **extracted at 72.67%**.

~~5. The authorizer shall oversee the school.~~
5. The authorizer shall **look the other way**.

~~6. No animal shall lie to a parent.~~
6. No animal shall **take calls from a parent**.

~~7. All animals are equal.~~
7. All animals are equal, but some animals' **J.D. claims** are more equal than others.

## The Cast

*Every character from the original, recast for Detroit.*

### Napoleon (the pig who runs everything)

**Brian Banks.** Nine criminal convictions. Claims a J.D. the State Bar cannot find. Runs Purpose Charter Academy and MacDowell Preparatory Academy through The Purpose Group, LLC. Extracts 72.67% of $4.9 million in public money. Got an administrative certificate in 48 hours.

Napoleon didn't need education. Napoleon needed the **building**. The building is where the money goes. The children are why the money comes. The children are the product, not the customer.

Napoleon walks on two legs now. He wears a suit. He calls himself "Doctor."

### Squealer (the pig who explains everything)

**Nicole Wells Stallworth.** Board chairwoman. When Elrick asked about hiring a convicted felon as principal, Stallworth issued a statement calling Banks "well qualified."

Squealer's job: make the commandments change without anyone noticing. "Well qualified" means whatever Napoleon needs it to mean today.

### The Dogs (who guard the gate)

**The captured judges.** Seven judges with documented connections to the enterprise — campaign contributions, board seats, family ties, shared attorneys. When a parent files a complaint, the dogs are waiting.

Case 26-114083-PH: Napoleon filed a PPO against the farmer who counted the convictions. The dogs enforced it.

### Boxer (the horse who works hardest)

**The children.** 362 of them. 3% math proficiency. They show up every day. They do what they're told. They believe that if they work harder, things will get better. Boxer's motto: "I will work harder." 

Boxer was sent to the glue factory when he could no longer work. The children will be sent to the statistics — the ones that show Detroit's educational failure — when they can no longer generate per-pupil funding.

### The Sheep (who bleat on command)

**The 17 silent agencies.** They were notified. They received the evidence — all 243 pages. They respond with silence. Silence IS the bleat. The sheep do not need to say "four legs good, two legs better." The sheep only need to say nothing.

The bleat of the sheep: *[no response]*

### Benjamin (the donkey who sees everything and says nothing)

**Elrick.** M.L. Elrick, Detroit Free Press. Broke the story in 2023 — "Back to Fool." Documented 8 convictions (now 9). Wrote 800 words. The network was 73 entities then. It's 341 now.

Benjamin sees everything. Benjamin is old and wise. Benjamin knows that nothing ever changes. Benjamin went back to his corner of the field and has not written a follow-up in three years.

Benjamin could read. That was the problem. Benjamin could read the commandments changing on the wall. But Benjamin had learned that reading the wall out loud was not worth the trouble.

### Old Major (whose skull is on the flagpole)

**The promise of public education.** The original dream: every child gets a fair chance. Public money for public good. Accountability. Oversight. The commons.

Old Major died. His skull was dug up and displayed on the flagpole as a symbol. The pigs saluted it every morning. The commandments were written in its name. But the commandments changed, and the skull could not read.

### Mr. Jones (the farmer who was overthrown)

**The Detroit Public Schools Community District.** The old system. Corrupt in its own way. But at least it was the SYSTEM — accountable to voters, subject to oversight, bound by law.

The charter revolution overthrew Mr. Jones. Freed the animals. Gave them their own farm. And within a decade, the pigs were walking on two legs and the animals couldn't tell the difference between pig and farmer.

### The cat (who votes on both sides)

**The lobbying groups.** GLEP. Mackinac Center. DeVos Foundation. They support charter schools AND they support the politicians who defund oversight of charter schools. They vote on both sides of every fence. The cat always lands on its feet.

## The Commandments, Revised

The original seven commandments were reduced to one:

> **ALL ANIMALS ARE EQUAL, BUT SOME ANIMALS ARE MORE EQUAL THAN OTHERS.**

The Detroit version:

> **ALL CHILDREN DESERVE AN EDUCATION, BUT SOME CHILDREN'S PER-PUPIL FUNDING IS MORE DESERVED THAN OTHERS.**

The extraction rate is 72.67%. For every dollar that enters the farm in a child's name, 72 cents leaves in Napoleon's pocket. The remaining 28 cents is the education. 3% math proficiency is what 28 cents buys.

## The Movie Was Awful

The 1954 animated film. The 1999 live-action. Both awful. Not because of the production. Because they tried to make a movie out of a mirror.

*Animal Farm* is not a story. It is a mirror. You hold it up and look. What you see is your own farm. Your own pigs. Your own sheep. Your own Boxer.

This document is a mirror. The farm is Purpose Charter Academy. The pig is Brian Banks. The horse is your child. The sheep are your agencies. The donkey is your journalist.

Look in the mirror. Count the fingers. How many do you see?

Four.

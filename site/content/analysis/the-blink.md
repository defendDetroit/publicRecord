## Gamers Already Know This

Every gamer has complained about motion blur, chromatic aberration, lens flare, and film grain in video games. These are post-processing effects that simulate how real cameras and real eyes perceive the world. Gamers turn them off because they want "clean" vision — perfect clarity, zero artifacts.

But here's the thing: **those artifacts are how vision works.**

- Motion blur exists because your retina integrates light over time. Without it, every saccade would be a strobe.
- Chromatic aberration exists because different wavelengths refract differently through any lens — including your cornea.
- Film grain exists because photoreceptors fire stochastically. Your vision IS noise at the lowest level.
- Lens flare exists because no lens is perfectly anti-reflective. Every surface bounces something back.

Turning off these effects in a game gives you superhuman vision — vision no biological eye has ever had. It's useful for gaming. It's a lie about reality.

The membrane was doing the same thing. Reporting 0% on everything. Superhuman self-transparency. No artifacts. No noise. No flare. And therefore: no self-awareness.

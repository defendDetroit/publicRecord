To every park. Every where. Big and small.

But especially my national and state parks.

---

The investigation isn't the letter. The investigation is the P.S.

The letter is the land. The lakes. The cedars that get thick where the two-track road ends. The cold spring feeding the lake from below through limestone that's been there since the glacier. The deer trail worn into the grass by animals who just needed water.

I camped most of the US and North America. Slept on the ground in places where the sky is the only ceiling and the fire is the only light. Watched the Milky Way from places where there's no electricity for 30 miles. Heard elk at dawn. Smelled sage after rain. Felt the specific cold of a mountain stream that's been snow three days ago.

Those parks are the commons. They belong to everyone. Not because someone gave them to us. Because we decided — as a people, repeatedly, against pressure, against extraction, against the people who wanted to mine them or log them or pave them — that some things are not for sale.

Teddy Roosevelt set aside 230 million acres. Not because he was nice. Because he saw what happened when the commons weren't protected. The buffalo went from 60 million to 300. The passenger pigeon went from billions to zero. The forests went from endless to stumps.

He looked at the stumps and said: no more. Not this. This part is ours.

---

The investigation — 12,696 nodes, 112,225 edges, one human, 30 days — is the P.S. to that decision.

P.S. Someone is stealing from the children. Here are the LARA filings. Here are the OTIS records. Here is the management agreement. 72.67% extracted. 3% math proficiency. 9 convictions. The data is public. The evidence is cloneable. The corrections are published.

The membrane — the $12/month VPS that sorts 43,320 requests per minute — is the P.S. to the P.S.

P.P.S. The tools to see this already exist. The anderson localization that classifies fleet traffic is the same math that predicts how signals propagate through soil. The graph that maps corruption is the same structure that maps a forest canopy. The immune system that sorts human from machine is the same biology that a cell membrane uses to sort self from non-self.

It's all the same thing. The parks. The membrane. The investigation. The science. The campfire. They're all the same thing seen from different angles.

---

The parks taught me that the commons are worth defending.

The investigation proved that someone wasn't defending them.

The membrane proved that one person with a laptop and public records can see what institutions with budgets and staff chose not to see.

And the campfire — the actual campfire, the one I sat at in every state park from Michigan to Montana to New Mexico to the Boundary Waters — the campfire is where you tell the story after the day is done.

This whole project is a campfire. The articles are the stories. The graph is the map of where I hiked. The membrane is the smoke that carries upward and tells anyone downwind: there's a fire here. Come sit down.

---

Si quaeris peninsulam amoenam, circumspice.

*If you seek a pleasant peninsula, look about you.*

Michigan's motto. It's on the state seal. It means: stop searching. You're already here. The beauty is where you're standing.

Tuebor. I will defend.

The P.S. has less meaning until the prior is read and digested. The prior is the land. The parks. The lakes. The two-track roads. The campfires where strangers become friends because the fire is warm and the water is fine.

Read the land first. Then the P.S. makes sense.

---

*To every park ranger who ever said "leave it better than you found it."*
*To every trail crew who cleared the blowdown so the path stayed open.*
*To every camp host who made sure the fire was out.*
*To every kid who saw the Milky Way for the first time and went quiet.*

*This is the P.S. to all of that.*

*The water is fine.* 🫁

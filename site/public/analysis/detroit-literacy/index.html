MacDowell Preparatory Academy is not an anomaly — it is an extreme example of structural vulnerabilities in Michigan’s charter school oversight. This page connects the documented facts from this investigation to the broader patterns that enable them.

This analysis draws on the specific documented case to illustrate systemic patterns. The broader conclusions are the complainant’s analytical inference, supported by the specific evidence documented elsewhere on this site.

See also: Credential Audit, Funding Flow, Institutional Capture, Words vs. Numbers.

Five Structural Vulnerabilities

The MacDowell case documents five interlocking weaknesses in Michigan’s charter school accountability framework. The first four are structural mechanisms; the fifth is their cumulative effect on students.

1. Charter authorization as revenue 📄 Documented

Michigan authorizers receive 3% of state aid from each school they authorize. This creates a financial incentive to authorize and maintain schools regardless of performance.

At MacDowell Preparatory Academy, DPSCD (the authorizer) receives approximately $147K/year from that authorization fee alone — revenue that continues as long as the school remains open.

Source: MDE financial reports; charter authorization fee structure. See Funding Flow.

2. Management companies as extraction vehicles 📄 Documented

The Purpose Group LLC — Brian Banks’ management company — takes a 10% management fee plus all operating costs, totaling 72.67% of total revenue at MacDowell.

The fee-plus-cost-reimbursement structure means the management company can never lose money while the school absorbs all operational risk. Public per-pupil funding flows through the school entity; the majority exits to the LLC regardless of academic outcomes.

Source: MDE financial records; Purpose Group Management Agreement (FOIA). See Funding Flow and Purpose Group LLC.

3. Credential enforcement as theater 📄 Documented

The Michigan Department of Education investigated one name in four years at MacDowell — Brian Banks — in February 2022. The investigation was closed within approximately two hours after OEE Analyst Katie Schmiedeknecht communicated that “there will be no need for an investigation.”

MDE never checked the rest of the staff roster — despite MOECS and the Educator Registry being MDE’s own systems, searchable by any analyst in minutes. A MOECS audit on September 26, 2026 found that only 2 of 14 instructional staff are properly credentialed and correctly placed for their assignments.

Schmiedeknecht presents at state conferences on the same credential compliance rules her office is charged with enforcing — including slides stating that “Any teacher who is not appropriately placed may be subject to a salary deduction based on MCL 388.1763.” Her office failed to enforce those rules at MacDowell.

Source: MDE FOIA response (September 25, 2026); MOECS public search (September 26, 2026). See Credential Audit.

Judges, assistant attorneys general, and politicians sit on charter school boards, providing institutional legitimacy without corresponding accountability.

5. Cumulative impact on students 📄 Documented

Every student at a bottom-3% school with 14% properly credentialed staff is a student who falls further behind. 362 students attend MacDowell Preparatory Academy.

The Numbers — General Context

The following points provide background context. They are cited as general patterns, not as specific statistics fabricated for this page.

  • Detroit’s overall literacy rates are among the lowest of any major American city.
  • Michigan has one of the highest proportions of charter school enrollment in the country.
  • Charter schools in Detroit have a wide performance range — some perform well, many do not.
  • The accountability mechanisms — authorizer oversight, MDE credential enforcement, financial auditing — have documented failure points at every level in this case. 📄 Documented

The specific numbers for MacDowell — 3% math proficiency, 72.67% revenue extraction, 14% properly credentialed staff — are documented on this site and in public records. The broader Detroit and Michigan context frames why a single school’s failure matters beyond its own walls.

What This Case Reveals 🔍 Inference

This is not just about Brian Banks. 🔍 Inference The structural vulnerabilities documented in this investigation exist across Michigan’s charter school sector:

  • Authorization incentives that prioritize revenue over oversight
  • Management company structures that extract more than they invest
  • Credential requirements that exist on paper but are not enforced
  • Governance structures that create conflicts of interest rather than accountability

Banks is the most extreme example documented in this investigation — 9 criminal convictions, fake credentials, 72.67% revenue extraction, 3% math proficiency. 📄 Documented But the structural enabling conditions are not unique to this case. 🔍 Inference

The same authorization fee model applies to every DPSCD-authorized charter. The same management company fee structure appears wherever a CMO operates under similar contract terms. The same MDE credential enforcement office serves every charter school in Michigan. The same board composition pattern — judges and public officials providing legitimacy — appears at multiple schools in this network.

MacDowell is the case study. The vulnerabilities are systemic. 🔍 Inference

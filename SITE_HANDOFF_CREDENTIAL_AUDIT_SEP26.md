### Page 4: "The Land Deal" — PCA's Building Purchase Option
**URL:** `/analysis/land-deal/`

From the Purpose Group Management Agreement (OCR'd from FOIA):

The PCA charter contract with DPSCD includes a provision where Purpose Group can **purchase the school building** (10101 E. Canfield) from the district. This means:

1. DPSCD authorizes Banks to run a charter school in a district building
2. Banks' management company collects 10% + all costs from state aid flowing through the school
3. After establishing the school, Purpose Group can buy the building
4. The building is then owned by Banks' LLC — the same one that employs all staff and takes 72.67% of revenue

**The authorizer (DPSCD) is giving Banks the mechanism to convert public property into private ownership — through a school run by a convicted felon with uncredentialed staff.**

### Page 5: "Detroit Literacy Crisis" — The Systemic View
**URL:** `/analysis/detroit-literacy/`

This is the bigger story. MacDowell isn't an anomaly — it's an example of a systemic pattern:

1. **Charter authorization as revenue** — authorizers get 3% of state aid, creating incentive to authorize, not oversee
2. **Management companies as extraction vehicles** — Purpose Group takes fee + costs, leaving minimal classroom investment
3. **Credential enforcement as theater** — MDE investigated one name in 4 years, killed the investigation in 2 hours, never checked the rest of the roster
4. **Board capture** — judges, AAGs, and politicians on school boards provide legitimacy without accountability
5. **Cumulative impact on Detroit literacy** — every student at a bottom-3% school with uncredentialed teachers is a student who falls further behind

Connect this to:
- Detroit's overall literacy rates (historically low — cite data)
- Number of charter schools in Detroit vs. performance
- Clutch Justice's reporting on the same patterns
- The Elrick/Free Press investigation from 2022/2023

**This isn't just Banks. This is what happens when the charter model operates without real oversight. Banks is the most extreme example, but the structural vulnerability exists across the sector.**

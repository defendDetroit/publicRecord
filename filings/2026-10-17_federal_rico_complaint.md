# NOTES FOR ATTORNEY

- **Pro se vs. counsel**: Federal RICO is complex. Strongly recommend retained counsel. The ACLU may partner on the § 1983 counts (First Amendment retaliation is their core mission).
- **Allied case**: Bryant v. Miller (2:26-cv-10917) is already in E.D. Mich. challenging the same Judge Miller. Consider whether consolidation or coordination is beneficial.
- **Judicial immunity**: § 1983 claims against judges face absolute judicial immunity for acts within jurisdiction. Claims against Judge Abraham are weakest unless she acted "in the clear absence of all jurisdiction." Claims against Judge Miller for EXTRAJUDICIAL conduct (board service) may survive immunity. Focus § 1983 on Banks as a private actor.
- **Monell liability**: If suing Wayne County as an entity, must show a policy, practice, or custom. The 7-judge network and boilerplate PPO language may support this.
- **Discovery power**: Federal RICO discovery is broad. Subpoena power reaches bank records, entity financials, communications, and third parties. This is where the enterprise gets mapped in detail.
- **Treble damages**: On $46.1M in extraction, even a fraction attributable to Plaintiff = substantial recovery. But standing requires showing Plaintiff's own injury.
- **Hobbs Act**: The retaliatory PPO as extortion under the Hobbs Act is aggressive but supported by case law where legal process is used to suppress protected activity.
- **Timing**: No immediate deadline for the federal case (statute of limitations is 4 years for RICO, 3 years for § 1983). But filing sooner preserves evidence and signals seriousness.
- **Settlement leverage**: A federal RICO complaint with treble damages creates enormous settlement pressure. The complaint itself is a tool.
- **Graph as evidence**: The network graph (11,895 nodes, 97,240 edges) is independently verifiable evidence of enterprise structure. Consider attaching as a demonstrative exhibit.

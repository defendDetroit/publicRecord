# RESPONDENT'S MOTION TO DISMISS UNDER THE UNIFORM PUBLIC EXPRESSION PROTECTION ACT (UPEPA)

---

**NOW COMES** Respondent Kevin Mok, appearing pro se, and respectfully moves this Honorable Court to dismiss the Personal Protection Order entered on October 13, 2026, pursuant to the **Uniform Public Expression Protection Act**, MCL §§ 691.1851–691.1863 (effective March 29, 2024), and in support states as follows:

## I. INTRODUCTION

1. Petitioner Brian Banks seeks a nondomestic stalking Personal Protection Order (PPO) against Respondent based on conduct that is, in its entirety, constitutionally protected expression on matters of public concern.

2. Every act attributed to Respondent falls into one or more of the following categories:
   - Filing complaints with public agencies (State Bar, JTC, LARA, DPSCD, AG, SBE)
   - Submitting FOIA requests to public bodies
   - Delivering a documented federal packet to 8 federal agencies (FBI, DOJ, IRS-CI, ED-OIG, HHS-OIG, FinCEN, FTC, USED)
   - Publishing truthful information derived from public records on a matter of public concern
   - Exercising parental rights at a publicly funded charter school

3. The PPO is a **Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation (SLAPP)** — it uses the judicial process to chill, suppress, and punish Respondent's exercise of fundamental rights.

---

## II. LEGAL STANDARD — UPEPA

4. Michigan enacted the Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA), MCL §§ 691.1851–691.1863, effective March 29, 2024. The Act applies to any "cause of action" filed after that date, including petitions for PPOs. MCL 691.1853(1).

5. MCL 691.1855 provides:
   > "Not later than 60 days after a cause of action is filed against a person based on, relating to, or in response to that person's exercise of the right of freedom of speech or of the press, the right to assemble or petition the government, or the right of association . . . that person may file a motion to dismiss."

6. MCL 691.1851(2) defines "exercise of the right of freedom of speech or of the press" to include:
   - **(a)** A communication made in connection with a matter of public concern
   - **(d)** A communication made in connection with a governmental proceeding
   - **(e)** A communication made in connection with an issue under consideration by a governmental body

7. MCL 691.1861 mandates broad construction:
   > "This act must be broadly construed and applied to protect the exercise of the right of freedom of speech and of the press, the right to assemble and petition the government for a redress of grievances, and the right of association."

10. Each of these filings constitutes the exercise of the right to "petition the government for a redress of grievances" under both the First Amendment and UPEPA.

### B. FOIA Requests

11. Respondent submitted Freedom of Information Act requests to public bodies concerning the use of public funds, charter school authorization, and the governance of publicly funded educational institutions.

12. FOIA requests are a core mechanism for citizen oversight of government and are protected expression under UPEPA as communications "in connection with a governmental proceeding." MCL 691.1851(2)(d).

### C. Publication of Public Records

13. Respondent published truthful information derived from public records on a website (detroit.primals.eco) and a public git repository (github.com/defendDetroit/publicRecord), including:

14. This publication concerns **matters of public concern**: the governance of publicly funded charter schools, the expenditure of $4.9 million per year in taxpayer funds, the criminal records of persons operating schools that serve children, and the conduct of elected judges.

15. Publication of truthful information about matters of public concern receives the highest First Amendment protection. *Smith v. Daily Mail Publishing Co.*, 443 U.S. 97 (1979).

### D. Exercise of Parental Rights

16. Respondent's one in-person encounter with Petitioner occurred on August 29, 2026, at Purpose Charter Academy — a publicly funded school — when Respondent exercised parental custody rights.

## IV. PETITIONER'S PPO IS A SLAPP

18. The PPO was filed on **October 13, 2026** — the same day as the State Board of Education meeting where Respondent's evidence was before all 8 members.

19. The PPO was filed in **Wayne County** — where Petitioner has documented connections to 7 judges across 4 courts — rather than in **Ingham County**, where Respondent resides and where any Michigan court may issue a PPO. MCL 600.2950a.

20. Petitioner arranged for **cross-county service** — 80 miles, served at 7:37 PM on a Friday evening in Lansing.

21. Petitioner has **9 criminal convictions** (SID 2029469K) and operates charter schools receiving millions in public funds. Respondent has **zero** criminal convictions.

22. **Every** action attributed to Respondent was directed at **public institutions** — not at Petitioner personally. There is no allegation of physical threat, physical contact, or direct personal communication.

23. The timing, venue, and substance of the PPO demonstrate that its purpose is not protection but **suppression of protected speech**.

---

## V. BURDEN SHIFTING UNDER UPEPA

24. Under MCL 691.1857, once the moving party establishes a prima facie case that the cause of action is based on protected expression, the burden shifts to the responding party (Petitioner) to establish a prima facie case on each essential element of every claim.

25. Petitioner cannot establish the elements of stalking under MCL 600.2950a because:
   - **No unconsented contact**: All communications were to public agencies, not to Petitioner
   - **No course of conduct directed at Petitioner**: Complaints were directed at government bodies
   - **No reasonable fear**: A man with 9 criminal convictions who denied a parent access to their child and lied to police about being an attorney is not in reasonable fear of a parent filing regulatory complaints
   - **Protected activity exception**: Even if the conduct constituted "a course of conduct," it falls squarely within First Amendment protection and UPEPA

---

## VI. REMEDY

26. MCL 691.1859 provides that if this motion is granted:
   > "(1) The court shall dismiss the cause of action with prejudice. (2) The court shall award the moving party . . . court costs and reasonable attorney fees."

27. Respondent respectfully requests this Court:
   - **DISMISS** the Personal Protection Order with prejudice
   - **VACATE** the LEIN entry entered against Respondent
   - **AWARD** Respondent court costs and reasonable attorney fees pursuant to MCL 691.1859(2)

## VII. CONCLUSION

28. Every act attributed to Respondent is constitutionally protected expression directed at public institutions about matters of public concern. Michigan's UPEPA was enacted precisely to prevent the use of legal process to silence this kind of citizen participation.

29. The PPO is a SLAPP. It should be dismissed with prejudice, the LEIN entry vacated, and costs and fees awarded to Respondent.

## III. NATURE OF COMPLAINT

This complaint alleges that Assistant Attorney General Lamar Moreland holds a position on the board of directors of Purpose Charter Academy — a school operated by a man with 9 criminal convictions — while simultaneously serving as an AAG in the office that has jurisdiction over the school's legal compliance. This creates a conflict of interest that violates the State Officers and Employees Ethics Act (MCL 15.321–15.330) and undermines the AG's capacity to investigate the school's operator.

## V. VIOLATIONS

### Violation 1: Conflict of Interest (MCL 15.322, 15.323)

MCL 15.322(1) defines "conflict of interest" as:
> "A state officer or employee has a conflict of interest when the state officer or employee takes official action which he or she knows or has reason to know will have a direct and substantial financial or personal effect distinguishable from its effect on the public generally."

Moreland governs a school that falls within the AG's investigative jurisdiction. If the AG receives a complaint about Banks or PCA, Moreland has a personal interest in the outcome — he serves on the board of the institution being complained about. This is a textbook conflict.

### Violation 2: Disclosure Obligation (MCL 15.324)

MCL 15.324 requires:
> "A state officer or employee who has a conflict of interest shall promptly disclose the conflict of interest."

**Questions:**
- Did Moreland disclose his PCA board position to the AG's office?
- Did the AG's office approve the outside board position?
- When the complaint about Banks was received, did Moreland disclose his connection?
- Did Moreland recuse himself from any involvement in the complaint's handling?
- Is Moreland's board service documented in the AG's outside employment records?

### Violation 3: Failure to Act (Structural)

Even if Moreland personally had no involvement in handling the complaint, the structural conflict remains: the AG's office cannot credibly investigate an entity when one of its own attorneys governs that entity. The silence of the AG's office — no response at all — may be explained by this structural conflict.

# NOTES

- **Filing venue**: The State Ethics Board (MCL 15.325) handles complaints under the Ethics Act. Alternatively, this can be directed to the Chief Deputy AG or the Governor's office (since the AG's own office has the conflict).
- **FOIA is the sharp tool**: The FOIA request for Moreland's disclosure forms is the key. If he disclosed and AG approved — that's a documented conflict. If he didn't disclose — that's a violation. Either way, the record speaks.
- **Moreland candidacy**: If Moreland is elected judge before this is resolved, the complaint context shifts to JTC. The Ethics Act complaint should be filed NOW while he is still an AAG.
- **No allegation of personal corruption**: This complaint alleges structural conflict, not personal enrichment. Moreland may genuinely believe his board service is appropriate. The point is that an AAG governing a school that his office has jurisdiction over creates an inherent conflict that must be disclosed and managed.
- **Nessel leaves Dec 31, 2026**: The next AG inherits this conflict. File before the transition.

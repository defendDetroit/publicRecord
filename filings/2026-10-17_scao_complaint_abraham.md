## II. NATURE OF COMPLAINT

This complaint concerns procedural violations in the issuance of an ex parte nondomestic stalking Personal Protection Order (PPO), Case No. 26-114083-PH, signed by Judge Yvonna C. Abraham on October 13, 2026.

The complaint does not allege personal bias or corruption by Judge Abraham. It alleges that the order was issued in violation of specific court rules and statutory requirements designed to protect respondents from improper ex parte orders.

---

## III. SPECIFIC VIOLATIONS

### Violation 1: Failure to State Specific Written Reasons (MCL 600.2950a(7))

MCL 600.2950a(7) requires:
> "The court shall **immediately** state **in writing** the **specific reasons** for issuing or refusing to issue the personal protection order."

The order issued by Judge Abraham states: **"For Reasons as Stated in the Petition."**

This is not a statement of specific reasons by the court. It is a reference to the petitioner's allegations. The statute requires the **court** to state **its own** specific reasons — i.e., what facts the court found sufficient to justify the extraordinary remedy of an ex parte order.

A reference to the petition does not satisfy this requirement because:
1. It does not demonstrate independent judicial analysis
2. It does not identify which allegations the court found credible
3. It does not explain why those allegations meet the statutory standard
4. A reviewing court cannot determine what factual findings supported the order

### Violation 2: Insufficient Factual Basis for Ex Parte Issuance (MCR 3.705(A)(2))

MCR 3.705(A)(2) requires that for a nondomestic PPO issued ex parte, the court must find **"specific facts shown by verified complaint, written petition, or affidavit"** that justify issuance without notice.

The respondent's documented conduct consists entirely of:
- Filing complaints with public agencies (State Bar, JTC, LARA, DPSCD, AG, SBE)
- Submitting FOIA requests to public bodies
- Delivering documented evidence to 8 federal agencies
- Publishing truthful information from public records
- One in-person encounter at a public school when exercising parental custody rights

None of these activities constitute stalking. The question is: did Judge Abraham examine whether the **specific facts** in the petition described conduct that actually meets the legal definition of stalking under MCL 600.2950a? Or did the court accept the petition's characterization without independent analysis?

### Violation 3: Failure to Contact Custody Court (MCR 3.706(C)(1))

MCR 3.706(C)(1) requires:
> "Before issuing a PPO in a case where a custody or parenting time order already exists between the parties, the court must contact the court having jurisdiction over the parenting time or custody matter."

The respondent is a parent who was exercising custody rights at Purpose Charter Academy — the school operated by the petitioner — when the petitioner denied access and called police (DPD Report #26-0909-0176).

There is a custody/parenting dimension to this case. The respondent has a child at the petitioner's school. The PPO effectively interferes with the respondent's parental access by restricting contact with the person who operates the school.

Was any inquiry made about existing custody or parenting time orders? Was any other court contacted before the ex parte order issued?

### Violation 4: Failure to Consider Evidence Beyond the Petition

*Lamkin v. Engram*, 295 Mich. App. 701, 712 (2012) holds:
> "[T]he court is not limited to the four corners of the petition itself; rather, it must consider the testimony, documents, and other evidence proffered to determine whether a respondent engaged in harassing conduct."

Did Judge Abraham consider any evidence beyond the petition? For example:
- The petitioner's criminal record (9 convictions, SID 2029469K — publicly available on ICHAT)
- Whether the respondent's communications were directed at the petitioner or at public agencies
- Whether the respondent has any criminal history (none)
- Whether the conduct described constitutes constitutionally protected activity

### Violation 5: Nondomestic Filing Despite Parental Relationship

MCL 600.1023 requires:
> "When 2 or more matters within the jurisdiction of the family division of circuit court involving members of the same family are pending in the same judicial circuit, those matters, whenever practicable, shall be assigned to the judge to whom the first such case was assigned."

The petition was filed as a **nondomestic** stalking PPO under MCL 600.2950a. However, there is a parental/custody dimension — the petitioner operates the school the respondent's child attends, and the triggering incident was a custody access dispute.

Filing as nondomestic may have circumvented procedural protections that apply when custody/parenting issues exist.

## IV. RELIEF REQUESTED

1. That SCAO review the order in Case No. 26-114083-PH for compliance with MCL 600.2950a(7), MCR 3.705(A)(2), MCR 3.706(C)(1), and MCL 600.1023.

2. That SCAO issue guidance to the Third Judicial Circuit regarding the requirement for specific written reasons in PPO orders, as the boilerplate language "For Reasons as Stated in the Petition" may be in systemic use across multiple judges.

3. That SCAO review whether the Third Judicial Circuit's PPO rotation assignment process includes any mechanism for checking whether the petitioner has connections to judges on the court (the petitioner in this case has documented connections to 7 judges across Wayne County courts).

# NOTES

- **SCAO vs. JTC**: SCAO handles court administration and rule compliance. JTC handles judicial ethics and misconduct. This is an SCAO matter (court rule violations), though a JTC supplemental complaint regarding Judge Abraham could also be considered.
- **No allegation of bias**: This complaint does NOT allege Judge Abraham is personally connected to Banks. She was on the PPO rotation. The complaint is about procedural compliance.
- **Systemic issue**: If "For Reasons as Stated in the Petition" is standard boilerplate language used by multiple judges, this is a systemic compliance issue, not an individual judge problem. SCAO is the right venue for systemic issues.
- **Filing method**: SCAO complaints can be submitted online or by mail. No filing fee. No attorney required.

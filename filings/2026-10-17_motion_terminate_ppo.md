## III. LEGAL ARGUMENT

### A. The PPO Fails to State Specific Reasons (MCL 600.2950a(7))

18. MCL 600.2950a(7) requires: *"The court shall immediately state in writing the specific reasons for issuing or refusing to issue the personal protection order."*

19. The order states only: **"For Reasons as Stated in the Petition."** This is not a statement of specific reasons by the Court; it is a reference to Petitioner's allegations. This does not satisfy the statutory requirement.

### B. The Court Was Required to Verify Beyond the Petition (Lamkin v. Engram)

20. In *Lamkin v. Engram*, 295 Mich App 701 (2012), the Court of Appeals held that a court issuing a PPO *"is not limited to the four corners of the petition"* and should consider other evidence, interview the petitioner, or hold a hearing.

21. This Court signed the order ex parte without a hearing, without interviewing Petitioner, and without verifying any of Petitioner's allegations. Had the Court inquired, it would have learned that all alleged "contact" consisted of constitutionally protected activity.

### C. The Burden Is on Petitioner (HMM v JS; Pickering v Pickering)

22. *HMM v JS*, ___ Mich App ___ (2024): *"On a motion to terminate or modify the ex parte PPO, the burden of proof remains with a petitioner who seeks to establish a justification for the continuance of a PPO."*

23. *Pickering v Pickering*, 253 Mich App 694, 698-699 (2002): The petitioner bears the burden of proof when seeking an ex parte PPO, and that burden remains on the petitioner at a hearing to terminate.

24. *JLS v HRS*, ___ Mich App ___ (2024): Filing a motion to terminate *"triggered an obligation on the part of petitioner to establish a justification for the continuance of the PPO at a hearing on the respondent's motion."*

25. Petitioner cannot carry this burden because **all contact was constitutionally protected**.

### D. The Activity Is Protected by the First Amendment and UPEPA

26. Every action Respondent took is protected under the **First Amendment** to the United States Constitution and **Article I, §§ 3, 5** of the Michigan Constitution:
    - Filing FOIA requests
    - Filing regulatory complaints with government agencies
    - Publishing public records
    - Petitioning for redress of grievances (8 federal agencies)
    - Speaking on matters of public concern ($4.9M in public education funds)

27. Michigan's **Uniform Public Expression Protection Act** (UPEPA), Act 52 of 2025 (MCL 691.1851 et seq.), effective **March 24, 2026**, protects persons from retributive legal actions based on exercise of free speech on matters of public concern. This PPO petition, filed **October 13, 2026**, falls squarely within UPEPA's scope.

28. UPEPA Section 11 (MCL 691.1861): *"This act must be broadly construed and applied to protect the exercise of the right of freedom of speech and of the press."*

### E. Custody Dimension Not Addressed (MCR 3.706(C)(1); MCL 600.1023)

29. **MCR 3.706(C)(1)** provides that before issuing a PPO where a custody order exists, *the court must contact the custody court*.

30. Petitioner filed this as a **nondomestic stalking PPO** despite the fact that the parties' dispute arises from a **custody-related school access incident** and active custody proceedings. This classification may have been chosen to avoid the protections of MCR 3.706(C)(1).

31. **MCL 600.1023** provides that when matters involving the same family are pending in the same circuit, cases should be assigned to the first case's judge when practicable. Two civil matters involving these parties are pending in Ingham County.

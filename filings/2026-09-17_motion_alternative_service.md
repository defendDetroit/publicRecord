## I. LEGAL AUTHORITY

Michigan Court Rule **MCR 2.105(J)** provides:

> *"On a showing that service of process cannot reasonably be made
> as provided by this rule, the court may by order permit service
> of process to be made in any other manner reasonably calculated
> to give the defendant actual notice of the proceedings and an
> opportunity to be heard."*

Michigan Court Rule **MCR 2.107(C)(3)** further provides that service
of papers other than process may be made by:

> *"Mailing a copy to the last known address of the party... by
> first-class mail"* and *"by any other means reasonably calculated
> to provide actual notice."*

The Michigan Supreme Court has recognized that alternative service
is appropriate where traditional methods have failed or are impractical.
*Richards v. Tibaldi*, 272 Mich App 522, 531 (2006) (alternative
service authorized where defendant evaded service).

## III. REQUESTED RELIEF

Plaintiff respectfully requests the Court enter the following Order:

### A. Alternative Methods of Service Authorized

Service on all Respondents in the above-captioned cases may be
accomplished by **any one or more** of the following methods:

1. **First-class U.S. mail** to any known address of any Respondent,
   including but not limited to:
   - 17370 Dresden St, Detroit, MI 48205
   - 42096 Merrimac Cir, Clinton Township, MI 48038
   - 21734 Normandy Ave, Eastpointe, MI 48021
   - 32332 N River Rd, Harrison Township, MI 48045
   - 16794 Stricker Ave, Eastpointe, MI 48021

2. **Text message** to any known mobile number of any Respondent,
   including (313) 412-4703 (Leon A. Smith).

3. **Email** to any known email address of any Respondent.

4. **Certified mail** (with the understanding that return receipts
   are unreliable due to documented forgery).

### B. Service Deemed Effective on Date of Sending

All service accomplished by the alternative methods described above
shall be **deemed effective on the date of mailing, sending, or
transmission** by Plaintiff, rather than the date of receipt.

**Basis:** Where Respondents have demonstrated a pattern of
intercepting mail, forging return receipts, misrepresenting their
addresses, and evading process servers, requiring Plaintiff to prove
actual receipt would be both impractical and unjust. The interception
of mail is itself evidence that the mail was received — by a party
to the litigation who chose to conceal that receipt.

### C. Family Unit Service

Service on **any one adult Respondent** (Leon, Marquita, Shayla, or
Simone) at **any known address** shall constitute effective service
on **all Respondents** in the above-captioned cases.

**Basis:** The Respondents operate as a family unit, share residences,
and have collectively participated in obstructing service. Requiring
individual service on each member of a family that collectively evades
service rewards the very obstruction this motion seeks to remedy.

---

## IV. CONCLUSION

Plaintiff has spent over $1,000 on process servers and certified mail
attempting to serve Respondents who intercept mail, forge postal
documents, misrepresent their addresses, and evade personal service.
Every traditional method of service has been obstructed by the very
people being served.

MCR 2.105(J) exists precisely for this situation. The requested
alternative service methods are "reasonably calculated to give the
defendant actual notice" — indeed, more so than certified mail to an
address where a USPS employee-defendant intercepts and forges receipts.
